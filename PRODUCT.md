# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Anyone with a day to fill and no desire to spend it deciding — solo, as a couple, with family, or with friends. Location is not a constraint on who this is for; the Delmarva content in the repo is where testing started, not who the product is for.

The defining context is low patience and low information. Someone opens Decide already slightly tired of the question, often with other people waiting on an answer. They are not researching, and they are not browsing for pleasure. They want the deciding to be over.

## Product Purpose

Decide plans a full day — where to eat, what to do, and in what order — and hands it over as a finished plan rather than a set of options.

Success is all three of these at once, and the product is not doing its job if it only manages one: the user escapes decision paralysis and goes; they end up somewhere they would not have found on their own; and they actually complete the day rather than abandoning it after the first stop.

## Positioning

Decide commits to a plan so you don't have to choose.

Everything else in this category hands over options and leaves the deciding to the user. Decide makes the call — one sequenced day, with a reason attached to each stop. Swapping a stop is an escape hatch, not the main event.

## Brand Personality

Knowing, direct, warm.

The voice is a well-travelled friend who actually knows the place, not a concierge reading from a script. It leads with a recommendation and explains why, rather than listing merits and retreating. It is willing to say a place is overrated, that the afternoon slot beats the morning, or that a stop is worth skipping — enthusiasm means something because it is selective. It treats the user as a capable adult who can handle a direct opinion, and it stays brief: a good three-sentence answer beats a padded ten-bullet list.

## Anti-references

- **A search results page.** Ten ranked options and no opinion. The listicle is the primary failure mode, and a plan that reads as a list has already failed.
- **A booking or OTA site.** TripAdvisor, Expedia and Yelp energy — ad-laden, upsell-driven, cluttered with badges, star ratings and sponsored placements.
- **A generic AI chat product.** Chat bubbles, sparkle iconography, "powered by AI" badges. The machinery is not the product and should never be the subject.
- **An enterprise dashboard.** Dense panels, chart grids and toolbars. Cold and operational, when this should feel like a recommendation from someone you trust.

## Design Principles

**Commit to an answer.** One plan, presented as a decision already made. Alternatives exist as escape hatches, never as the primary interface. Any screen that fans out into a comparison grid has reverted to the thing this product replaces.

**Every recommendation carries its reason.** A stop without a "why" is indistinguishable from a search result. The reason is what makes the plan trustworthy enough to act on, and it is what earns the right to be opinionated.

**The day is a sequence, not a set.** Order, timing, travel between stops and the shape of the day are the product. Anything that presents stops as an unordered collection discards the actual value.

**Accommodation shapes the output, not just the settings.** Sensory, dietary and access needs already change what gets recommended rather than filtering a generic result afterwards. Treat that as core behaviour to protect, not a preferences feature.

**Never sound like software.** No system vocabulary, no hedging, no exposed machinery. If a sentence could have come from any app in the category, it is wrong for this one.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target: **WCAG 2.2 AA.** Body text at 4.5:1, UI components and large text at 3:1, verified in both light and dark appearances rather than assumed from the light one.

The product already leans inclusive and that should be preserved and extended: a sensory-friendly bias for itineraries, a per-spin sensory-friendly toggle, stored sensitivities covering both food allergens and environmental triggers, and light/dark/auto theming. Contrast must hold in both themes — the failure mode observed in practice is a control that is legible in one appearance and invisible in the other.
